Tonight I would make the whole closing Bible Study feel like **a graduation lecture from the “University of Faith”** without becoming sentimental. The surprise is that after all these weeks, we discover something unsettling: # **DAVID NEVER GRADUATED FROM FORMATION.** He became king. He became successful. He became established. He grew old. And God was **still shaping him**. That is where I would take the room. # FINAL BIBLE STUDY 2025/26 # **FORMED TO FINISH** ### *David Beyond the Throne* **Main movements: 2 Samuel 24 → 2 Samuel 6 → 2 Samuel 7 / 1 Chronicles 22, 28–29 → Acts 13:36** ### Central question # **What does a lifetime of formation finally produce?** Not perfection. A heart that can still: - recognise when it is wrong, - worship when people misunderstand, - accept God's “No,” - prepare another person to succeed, - and finish its own generation faithfully. --- # OPENING: “LET US BEGIN AT THE END” Say: > “For months we have walked with David. Tonight I want to do something unusual. I want to begin near the end of his life, and then walk backwards through three scenes. Because perhaps we thought formation was about getting David to the throne. Tonight we will discover that God was still forming David long after he reached it.” Then very quickly remind them of the journey. ### 1 Samuel 16:13 **Anointed.** ### 1 Samuel 18:10–11 **Attacked.** ### 1 Samuel 25:32–35 **Corrected by Abigail.** ### 1 Samuel 30:6 **Strengthened himself in God.** ### 2 Samuel 1:11–12 **Mourned the enemy who had hunted him.** ### 2 Samuel 2:1 **Asked God before moving.** ### 2 Samuel 5:3 **Finally established as king.** Then say: > “We might have finished the series there and said, ‘Mission accomplished. David has arrived.’ But Scripture keeps going.” # **The throne was not the end of David’s formation.** That is tonight. --- # SCENE ONE: THE CENSUS ## **When success becomes something you can count** ### 2 Samuel 24:1–10 Tell them: > “Let us begin near the end. David is no longer the boy running from Saul. He is now an established king with armies, victories, territory and influence.” Then: ### 2 Samuel 24:2 David commands Joab to number Israel. And Joab questions him. ### 2 Samuel 24:3 Even Joab, of all people, senses something troubling here. That itself should make the class smile: > “When Joab becomes your voice of caution, perhaps you really should listen.” Then: ### 2 Samuel 24:4 > “Notwithstanding the king’s word prevailed…” David insists. --- # BUT WHY WAS THE CENSUS WRONG? This must be clear. Tell them: > “Counting people was not automatically sinful.” God commanded censuses elsewhere. ### Numbers 1:1–3 God Himself orders Israel to be numbered. So the sin cannot simply be: > **“Never count people.”** Otherwise church membership statistics are about to become a very tense subject. The exact issue is not spelled out in one simple sentence. But Scripture clearly tells us: ### 2 Samuel 24:10 David later confesses: > **“I have sinned greatly…”** And the parallel account says: ### 1 Chronicles 21:1 Satan stood against Israel and provoked David to number Israel. There is also the difficult theological statement in **2 Samuel 24:1**, where the narrative places the event within God's judgment on Israel. Do not get lost there tonight. The two accounts together show that God remains sovereign while Satan incites David, and David remains responsible for his own action. The spiritual danger appears to involve David's relationship to **strength, military power and security**. So put the question to them: # **Why does a king need to know exactly how many fighting men he has?** Perhaps because numbers can become reassurance. Earlier David had to ask: > “Lord, will You deliver me?” Now he has enough soldiers to ask: > “How strong am I?” There is the danger. # **The test changes when you finally have something to count.** --- # MAKE IT PRACTICAL Our census may not be soldiers. We count: - bank balance, - members, - supporters, - qualifications, - contacts, - followers, - assets, - achievements. None is automatically wrong. But ask: # **When does something God gave me quietly become what I trust instead of God?** That is the issue. And this punchline: # **It is possible to receive a blessing from God and eventually begin trusting the blessing more than the God who gave it.** Formation must continue even after success. --- # THEN DAVID’S HEART DOES SOMETHING ## 2 Samuel 24:10 > **“David’s heart smote him…”** Stop. Compare this David with earlier David. ### With Nabal Someone had to come and stop him. 📖 **1 Samuel 25:32–35** Abigail had to stand in the road. But here: > **David's own heart smites him.** No Abigail. No Nathan yet. No Samuel. Something has been formed inside the man. # **Formation is when correction that once had to chase you begins living inside you.** That is deep. Earlier: > Somebody had to tell David, “Stop.” Now: > Something within David says, “This is wrong.” Not because David has become perfect. But because his conscience has become sensitive. ### 2 Samuel 24:10 He says: > **“I have sinned greatly…”** And this is a beautiful definition: # **A formed person is not someone who never gets it wrong. A formed person increasingly becomes unable to remain comfortable when they know they are wrong.** --- # NOW THE THRESHING FLOOR ## 2 Samuel 24:18–25 This also helps your sermon preparation without hijacking tonight's Bible Study. God sends Gad to David. ### 2 Samuel 24:18 > Build an altar on the threshing floor of Araunah. Araunah offers David everything. Land. Oxen. Wood. Free. Then David says: ### 2 Samuel 24:24 > **“Neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing.”** Do not make tonight mainly about repentance. Make it about formation: # **The David who once took what belonged to other people has become a David who refuses to offer God something that costs him nothing.** That is movement. And then tell them something surprising. ### 1 Chronicles 21:26 David builds the altar there, and God answers with fire. ### 1 Chronicles 22:1 David says: > **“This is the house of the LORD God…”** And later: ### 2 Chronicles 3:1 Solomon builds the temple on **Mount Moriah**, at the site associated with Ornan/Araunah's threshing floor. Now there is your wow moment: # **The place where David was corrected became connected to the place where another generation would worship.** Do not say God needed David's sin to produce the temple. He did not. But grace can take a place of failure, correction and surrender and refuse to let that place be wasted. # **God can make yesterday’s place of correction become tomorrow’s place of worship.** Then leave the threshing floor. We are going somewhere else. --- # TRANSITION Say: > “But perhaps formation is not only visible when David gets something wrong. What happens when David is doing something right, but somebody thinks he looks ridiculous?” Now go backwards. # SCENE TWO: THE KING WHO DANCED ## 2 Samuel 6:12–23 Before Michal, briefly remind them of Uzzah. ### 2 Samuel 6:3–7 They initially transport the Ark on a new cart. Uzzah touches it and dies. Then later David discovers: ### 1 Chronicles 15:13 > **“We sought him not after the due order.”** So David learns: # **Good intentions do not make God's instructions unnecessary.** That is formation too. First attempt: **Excitement without proper order.** Second attempt: **Joy with obedience and reverence.** Then: ### 2 Samuel 6:14 > “David danced before the LORD with all his might…” Now enter Michal. --- # MICHAL AT THE WINDOW ## 2 Samuel 6:16 She sees: > David leaping and dancing. And: > **“she despised him in her heart.”** You can make them laugh here. Say: > “David finishes worship, blesses the people, gives everybody food, and probably thinks, ‘What a service!’ Then he arrives home.” And there is Mmamoruti Michal. ### 2 Samuel 6:20 Her greeting is essentially: > **“What a distinguished performance from His Majesty today!”** Not exactly, “Welcome home, darling, worship was powerful.” 🤣 She attacks his dignity. “How glorious was the king of Israel today... uncovering himself...” --- # WAS DAVID NAKED? No. ### 2 Samuel 6:14 David was wearing a **linen ephod**. Michal's criticism concerns his perceived lack of royal dignity. She believes: > “Kings do not behave like this.” She grew up as **Saul's daughter**. She knows royal protocol. Distance. Image. Majesty. Now David is dancing among ordinary people. And she despises it. --- # THEN DAVID GIVES THE ANSWER ## 2 Samuel 6:21 > **“It was before the LORD…”** That is enough. # **Michal was watching the king. David was watching the Lord.** She saw: > “How do you look?” David saw: > “Who am I worshipping?” Then David reminds her: > God chose me before your father and his house. He is not literally saying: > “That is why there is no king in your family.” But he is reminding her that **his kingship itself came from God**. Then: ### 2 Samuel 6:22 David says he is prepared to become even more undignified/humbled. The idea is: > “If worshipping God makes me look small, I am prepared to look small.” And there is your formation line: # **The crown had reached David’s head, but it had not yet made him too important to dance before God.** That is dangerous territory for leaders. Before promotion: > “Lord, use me!” After promotion: > “Please remember my title.” Before the position: > worship freely. After the position: > “People are watching.” David says: # **“It was before the LORD.”** --- # WHY WAS THE DANCING NECESSARY? Not because Scripture commands every believer to dance exactly as David danced. The significance is that David's worship expresses: - joy, - humility, - gratitude, - abandonment before God. The Ark, representing God's covenant presence, is finally coming into Jerusalem. David knows: > **The true glory of Jerusalem will not be David sitting on a throne. It will be God's presence among His people.** That is deep. # **The king understood that the King was not the most important person in Jerusalem.** There. That is the point. David could wear a crown because he knew there was still Someone before whom he must bow. --- # BE CAREFUL WITH MICHAL’S CHILDLESSNESS ### 2 Samuel 6:23 Michal has no child until her death. Do not preach confidently: > “God made her barren because she mocked David.” The text does not explicitly tell us the precise causal explanation. What it does show is a tragic relational end to the scene. Keep the text clean. --- # NOW TRANSITION TO THE FINAL STORY Say: > “David has learned that the crown must never become too heavy for worship. But there is still one more level of formation. Can David worship when God says ‘No’?” Now the room should go quiet. # SCENE THREE: DAVID’S DREAM ## 2 Samuel 7:1–3 David finally has rest. ### 2 Samuel 7:1 God has given him rest from his enemies. He sits in a cedar house. Then: ### 2 Samuel 7:2 David says: > **“I dwell in an house of cedar, but the ark of God dwelleth within curtains.”** Look how far David has come. His thought is: > **“God has been good to me. I want to do something great for God.”** He wants to build the temple. This is not a sinful desire. It is beautiful. Even Nathan says: ### 2 Samuel 7:3 > “Go, do all that is in thine heart…” Everybody thinks: > Fantastic idea. Then God speaks. --- # AND GOD SAYS NO ### 2 Samuel 7:4–5 God sends Nathan back. David will not be the one to build the house. Then Chronicles later gives the fuller explanation. ### 1 Chronicles 22:7–10 David says God told him: > **“Thou hast shed blood abundantly, and hast made great wars…”** His son Solomon, a man associated with rest, would build it. Now stop. # **How do you respond when God says “No” to something you wanted to do FOR GOD?** That is different. This is not: > “David wants to commit sin and God says no.” No. David wants to honour God. And God still says: > **“Not you.”** That is mature formation. --- # DOES THIS MEAN GOD NEVER FORGAVE DAVID? Absolutely not. Make this very clear. ### 2 Samuel 12:13 After David's sin concerning Bathsheba and Uriah, Nathan says: > **“The LORD also hath put away thy sin…”** Forgiveness is explicit. And in **2 Samuel 7**, when God tells David he will not build the temple, God simultaneously gives David one of the greatest promises in Scripture. ### 2 Samuel 7:8 > “I took thee from the sheepcote…” ### 2 Samuel 7:9 > “I was with thee…” ### 2 Samuel 7:16 > “Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established…” This is not an unforgiven man whom God has rejected. So teach: # **Forgiveness and assignment are not the same thing.** God can forgive me fully and still say: > **“That particular assignment is not yours.”** And consequences or limitations do not automatically mean forgiveness was withheld. David's history as a warrior king mattered to the assignment. The temple, associated with God's dwelling and peace, would be built by Solomon, a man whose reign would be characterised by rest. 📖 **1 Chronicles 22:9–10** --- # NOW WATCH DAVID’S RESPONSE This is where tonight should explode. David could have said: > “If I cannot build it, nobody is getting my money.” 🤣 He could have told Solomon: > “Since God chose you, let God buy your materials.” He could have become bitter. He could have stopped giving. He could have withdrawn. He could have said: > **“That was MY vision!”** But: ### 1 Chronicles 22:5 > **“David prepared abundantly before his death.”** Come on. # **God said, “You will not build it.” David said, “Then I will prepare the one who will.”** That is formation. --- # GO DEEPER ## 1 Chronicles 22:6–16 David calls Solomon. He tells him God's plan. Then: ### 1 Chronicles 22:11 > **“Now, my son, the LORD be with thee…”** He blesses the person who will do what he wanted to do. # **Can I pray for somebody to succeed in the place where God told me I cannot go?** That is mature Christianity. --- # THEN DAVID HANDS HIM THE PLANS ## 1 Chronicles 28:11–20 David gives Solomon plans. Resources. Organisation. Instructions. Then: ### 1 Chronicles 28:20 David says: > **“Be strong and of good courage, and do it…”** Now pause for a long time. # **The man God told, “You cannot do it,” tells his son, “You do it.”** That may be the deepest line tonight. Because immature formation says: > “If it cannot be me, I don't want it.” A formed heart says: > **“If this is God's will, I want it to succeed even without my name on it.”** --- # AND THEN DAVID GIVES HIS OWN MONEY ## 1 Chronicles 29:2–5 David gathers resources. Then: ### 1 Chronicles 29:3 He says: > **“I have set my affection to the house of my God…”** And he gives from his personal treasure. He will not build it. He will not see the final building completed. Yet he invests in somebody else's future. # **Formation is when I can finance a vision that will not carry my name.** That is leadership maturity. Parents. Pastors. Lay speakers. Class leaders. Church officers. Older members. Can I prepare someone to do what I will no longer be around to do? --- # NOW GIVE THEM THE THREE STAGES OF MATURE FORMATION Put this up: ## **THE CENSUS** ### Can God still correct me after I become successful? 📖 2 Samuel 24:10 ## **THE DANCE** ### Can I remain humble after I receive the crown? 📖 2 Samuel 6:14, 21 ## **THE DREAM** ### Can I remain faithful when God gives my desired assignment to somebody else? 📖 1 Chronicles 22:5; 28:20 That is a very clean final Bible Study. --- # NOW BRING THE WHOLE YEAR TOGETHER Say: > “When we began David, we thought formation was about getting him ready for kingship. But tonight we know better.” Formation taught David: ### In the cave: **Do not force it.** ### With Nabal: **Receive correction.** ### At Ziklag: **Seek God before acting.** ### At Saul's death: **Let God change your heart, not only your actions.** ### At Hebron: **Wait for fullness.** ### With the Ark: **Do God's work God's way.** ### Before Michal: **Never become too important to worship.** ### At the census: **Do not replace God with what you can count.** ### With the temple: **Accept when your role is to prepare somebody else.** Now ask: # **What has God been forming in me this year?** Not: > “What Bible Studies did I attend?” Not: > “How many notes did I write?” But: # **Am I responding differently?** Because that has been your definition all along. # **Formation becomes visible when familiar pressure begins producing a different response.** --- # THEN GO TO THE FINAL SCRIPTURE OF DAVID’S LIFE Now here is your close. Do not close in Kings with simply: > “David died.” Go to the New Testament interpretation of his life. ### Acts 13:36 > **“For David, after he had served his own generation by the will of God, fell on sleep…”** Stop. # **He served his generation.** He was not perfect. But he served. He failed. But he served. He was corrected. But he served. He waited. He worshipped. He prepared Solomon. Then: > **he fell asleep.** What a way to end the year. --- # “SERVED HIS GENERATION” Explain it. David did not serve every generation. He did not build everything. He did not finish everything. He did not become everything. But: # **He served the generation God gave him.** That is enough. This is liberation for leaders. You do not need to do everything. You need to faithfully do **your part**. David wanted to build the temple. God said: > “Your son.” And David learned: # **My part is preparation. His part is construction.** Both serve God's purpose. That is beautiful. --- # THE LAST GREAT FORMATION LESSON # **Formation teaches me to know my assignment without needing every assignment.** That is why David can die. He does not have to stay alive until Solomon finishes. He has served **his generation**. Another generation must now rise. # **A formed person knows when to wait, when to act, when to worship, when to prepare, and eventually when to release.** --- # YOUR HIGH CLOSE Slow everything down. Say: > “Beloved, perhaps the greatest lesson David has taught us this year is that formation never meant becoming perfect. David was anointed and still needed formation. David became king and still needed formation. David became older and still needed formation. He could get it right today and need correction tomorrow.” Then: # **The oil did not finish him.** # **The throne did not finish him.** # **Success did not finish him.** # **Age did not finish him.** # **God kept forming David until David had served his generation.** Then ask: > “What has changed in me since we began this journey?” Then slowly: # **Am I easier to correct?** # **Am I slower to retaliate?** # **Do I ask God before I force things?** # **Can I celebrate another person's assignment?** # **Can God say no to me without losing my worship?** # **Can I prepare somebody else to go further than me?** That is formation. --- # THEN YOUR FINAL THREE LINES FOR THE WHOLE SERIES # **Wait for what God has promised.** David learned that. # **Carry faithfully what God has given.** David learned that. # **Release faithfully what God gives to the next generation.** David finally learned that too. Then: ### Acts 13:36 > **“David… served his own generation by the will of God, [and] fell on sleep.”** And now your last line for Bible Study 2025/26: # **May we not merely finish the year older. May we finish it more formed.** Pause. Then: # **And when our assignment is finished, may heaven be able to say: they served their generation by the will of God.** That is where I would stop. No extra story after that. Let **Acts 13:36** have the final word.
